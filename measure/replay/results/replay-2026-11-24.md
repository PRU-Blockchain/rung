# Rung replay on real pump.fun trading: three scenarios (2026-11-24)

Every number below comes from real mainnet trades fetched with read-only JSON-RPC calls (the public `api.mainnet-beta.solana.com` for the candidate scans, and an authorized keyed endpoint serving the same methods for most of the window data) and cached under `measure/replay/cache/`. Two kinds of numbers appear, and each is labelled:

- **[chain]** measured directly from transactions: trade counts, failed transactions and their error codes, prices, reserves, typed slippage bounds, sandwiches.
- **[sim]** simulated on the real tape: what a fixed-slippage buy or a Rung order *would* have done if placed at the moment of each real buy, with that buy's SOL amount. The tape itself is not changed.

Headline setting: quote age k = 3 slots (price seen 3 slots before the order lands), Rung first try 5 slots after the order (first try sent together with the order, measured: 3, 4, 4 and 10 slots on mainnet with the script, 5 on devnet with the app; the old sender's median was 9), later tries 4 slots apart. One slot ≈ 0.215 s in this data. Sensitivity tables follow each scenario.

## S1. Hyped launch

**How the tokens were chosen [chain]:** all pump.fun curves that graduated (migrated to PumpSwap) in the 24 hours before the scan anchor, found through pump's `withdraw_authority` (every migration references it), plus the tokens named in `tokens.json`; the 40 with the most successful curve-account transactions in the first 60 s were re-ranked by successful transactions on the curve's token account (every trade moves it; see the note under the table) and the top 12 were taken (SOL volume was not used for ranking: it needs every transaction). Window: the first 60 s after creation (or to the curve's completion if earlier). These launches run at 30–60 successful curve transactions per second and most of those are bots' no-op checks that never call pump, which a signature list cannot tell apart from trades. So every block of the window is read (`getBlock` with `transactionDetails: "accounts"`: each transaction's accounts and balances before/after, no instruction data) and every transaction that changed the curve's token balance is a trade, in block order, with exact reserves (balances + offsets calibrated on pump's TradeEvent in a fully fetched sample of 120 transactions). Typed slippage is known only for the sampled trades. The dev buy inside the create transaction is not in the tape. Prices 1, 5 and 10 min later come from a 30 s grid after the window.

## Method and limits

**Data [chain].** `scan-graduates.mjs` lists migrations through pump's `withdraw_authority` and pages each graduate's bonding-curve signatures (`getSignaturesForAddress`, which also gives each transaction's index in its block and the error code of failed ones). `fetch-scenarios.mjs` chooses the windows (rules above, fixed before looking at any replay result) and fetches every successful transaction in them (`getTransaction`, `maxSupportedTransactionVersion: 1`), plus one transaction every 15 s (60 s in S3) for 11 min after each window for part E. `build.mjs` decodes the trades; `sim.mjs` replays. Read-only calls only (`getSignaturesForAddress`, `getTransaction`, `getBlock`, `getAccountInfo`, `getMultipleAccounts`, `getTokenSupply`, `getSlot`), paced with back-off; build and sim run strictly from the cache (`node --import ./tools/offline.mjs …` stops on any network request).

**Decoding.** Curve: pump's TradeEvent (SOL and token amounts, virtual reserves after the trade, fee rates) paired with the buy instruction (typed bound). Pool: reserves from the pool's base and quote token-account balances before/after, plus PumpSwap's `virtual_quote_reserves` read from the pool account at fetch time; fee rates and the buyer's total spend from PumpSwap's BuyEvent, used only when the event's reserves equal the balances.

**Reconstruction checks [chain vs formula].** Quote formula (client/src/quote.ts) on the reconstructed pre-trade state vs tokens actually received, every replayed buy: curve buys n=5793, median |error| 1.93e-9, p99 2.99e-5, within 0.01%: 100.0%; pool buys n=661, median 2.95e-4, p99 1.29e-3, within 0.01%: 20.4%. Execution order: 2479 slots chained by reserves, 15 fell back to the RPC's transaction index; 0 chained pairs disagreed with the transaction index; 15 reserve gaps in the tapes; 1 transactions the RPC did not return.

**Slot length [chain].** Block time over slot number in the tapes: median 0.215 s per slot (p10–p90 0.212–0.217 s, 59 windows; block times have 1 s resolution). So 4 slots between Rung tries is about 0.9 s, and a quote age of 3 slots is about 0.6 s.

**Block reads (S1) [chain].** 2741 blocks; 88,364 transactions touched the curves, 63,683 of them failed, and 7,117 changed the curve's token balance (trades). In the fully fetched samples, 891 of 1287 successful transactions did not trade. Cross-check against pump's own TradeEvent for 385 sampled trades: token amount and virtual tokens identical in 385; virtual SOL (from k / virtual tokens) within 6.7e-9 relative; k varied by at most 1.3e-8 across samples. The curve account's lamports are not used for reserves: on the curve checked by hand (tools/check-offsets.mjs) its lamport change differs from the event's SOL amount by 0.775% of it on both buys and sells, i.e. part of the fee stays in the curve account.
**No-op share elsewhere [chain].** Of the successful transactions fetched in full in S2/S3 windows, 7,669 touched the venue without trading (bots' conditional transactions), next to 3,747 trades.

**Explorer-style decoder check [chain] (tools/check-decoder.mjs, S2 and S3 tapes).** 240 curve trades: decoded token amount equals the change of the curve's token-account balance in 240; the decoded SOL amount equals the curve account's lamport change in 68 (in the others the curve also keeps a fee share, 0.775% of the amount). Pool buys: PumpSwap's BuyEvent reserves equal the pool token-account balances in 661 of 661.

**Simulation assumptions [sim].**
1. Quote age k ∈ {1, 3, 5}: the user saw the venue at the end of slot (buy slot − k). Buys in a window's first k slots of a fresh curve "saw" the reserves before the window's first trade.
2. The fixed buy and Rung's order land exactly where the real buy landed (state just before it). Rung's `start` is computed there.
3. Rung try i lands at buy slot + d + 4·i with d ∈ {1, 3, 5, 9} (d = 9 is the old sender), against the state at the start of that slot. A try due after the curve completed fails ("curve completed"). Tries past the fetched data count as not filled and are counted (`unknown` in the JSON).
4. Each counterfactual buy is alone: it does not move the tape; other traders (including sandwich bots) act as they did. A bot facing a tighter bound would push less or skip, so the sandwich section shows what the bound allowed, not how bots would adapt.
5. Spend = the real buy's total (SOL into the venue + fees). Fee rates from the trade's own event; curve events without fee fields (older layout): 1%; pool buys without a readable BuyEvent: 0.20% + 0.05% + 0.05%.
6. "Room to a sandwich bot" = tokens obtainable / minimum accepted − 1, in SOL spend × (1 − minimum/obtainable): an upper bound of what a sandwich could take from that buy, not money anyone took. For own typed slippage it is measured (what the instruction allowed vs what the buy got). For Rung the first reading uses the filled try's bound; the second assumes a bot also front-runs the order-opening transaction, which lowers `start`, so only the cap protects. The first reading is what one same-block sandwich around the try that fills can take. The second needs a separate, earlier manipulation: a buy placed before the order-opening transaction (it can be sold right after it, even in the same block, because `start` stays stored) that the bot pays round-trip fees on without any Rung buy to profit from at that moment, followed by a sandwich around the later try; it is not a single same-block sandwich, but it also does not require holding the position for d slots. When the price fell between the quote and the landing, a fixed X% buy has more than X% of room (the bound is set from the older, higher price); that is why mean room can exceed the nominal slippage in S1.
7. Part E uses the spot price (virtual quote / virtual tokens) from the tape and the 15-second (S3: 60-second) grid; after a curve completed, the last curve price stands in.

**Reproduce.** From `measure/replay/` (Node from `client/`'s toolchain, no extra dependencies): `node discover.mjs` → `HOURS=24 node scan-graduates.mjs` → `node scan-ata.mjs` → `N_S1=12 S1_SEC=60 SAMPLE_S1=120 N_S2=14 CAP_S2=600 S3_WINDOWS=10 CAP_S3=250 node fetch-scenarios.mjs plan` → `node fetch-scenarios.mjs fetch S1` (then `S2`, `S3`) → `node build.mjs` → `node sim.mjs` → `node tools/check-decoder.mjs 20`. Every RPC response is cached under `cache/` (block reads as per-slot extracts in `cache/blocks/`), so a rerun with the cache present makes no requests and reproduces these tables exactly; `scan-anchor.json` and `s3-anchor.json` pin "now" for the candidate lists and the calm windows.

**Not covered / limits.** Only the first CAP successful transactions of very busy windows are replayed (marked "first N s"); fees paid by failed transactions are not counted; PumpSwap pool windows use the pool's current `virtual_quote_reserves` for the whole window; sandwich detection is pattern-based (order + wallet or matched amount, same or next slot): a lower bound for multi-wallet bots, and a sell that happens to match an amount can be a false positive; graduates with more than 25,000 curve signatures were not ranked; 2024 tokens that graduated to Raydium were not used.
